# Hindi course: items for a native-speaker review

Nothing below is known to be wrong. These are choices between accepted variants, or points where usage is
divided, that a Hindi-speaking reviewer should confirm.

## Romanisation choices (whole course)

- Short `a` next to ह is kept as `a` (bahan, pahle, kahte, rahte, shahar, mahangaa, bahut), as in the
  existing course, although it is pronounced like `e` (behen, pehle, kehte). This is explained in the
  `about` section "Where the sound differs from the spelling". If speech scoring compares against what a
  recogniser transcribes, these words may deserve `e`.
- यह / वह are romanised `yah` / `vah` (existing scheme) though normally said `ye` / `vo`; छह is `chhah`
  though said `chhe`. Same remark.
- Retroflex and dental consonants are not distinguished (existing scheme); ड़ = `r`, ढ़ = `rh`.
- इ + ए is written `iye` everywhere (keejiye, aaiye, diye, liye, piyenge).
- इंतज़ार is `intazaar` (also heard as intezaar / intzaar). कृपया is `kripayaa`. रुपये is `rupaye`.
  मूँगफली is `moongphalee`. कंपनी is `kampnee`, भारतीय is `bhaarteey`, बहनें is `bahnen` (schwa dropped).

## Spelling choices (whole course)

- Nukta is written on ज़ and फ़ only. क़, ख़, ग़ are shown in the letter table but lesson words are spelt
  without the dot (खबर, गलत, कीमत, वक्त, खुशी, खराब, खाली, तारीख, बुखार, खत्म). Explained in `about`.
- हिंदी in the lessons, हिन्दी as `nativeName` (carried over) and in the conjunct table.
- बिलकुल (03-polite-words) rather than बिल्कुल; the note gives the other spelling.
- गर्म rather than गरम; रुपये rather than रुपए; मेनू rather than मेन्यू; दुख rather than दुःख.

## Individual items

- 00-course, letters: अं, अँ, अः are listed under "Other signs", not under "Vowels" (the validator rejects
  a letter listed twice, and warns when the example does not contain the letter). क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ, श्र are under
  "Consonants" and therefore not repeated under "Common conjuncts".
- 00-course, Other signs: the example for the halant (क्) is क्या, where the halant is not visible. A word
  with a printed halant would be rarer and bookish.
- 01-greetings, "शुभ रात्रि": kept from the existing course, with a note that it is bookish.
- 02-introductions, "मैं यहाँ घूमने आया हूँ।" translated "I am here on a visit" (kept); literally "I have
  come here to travel around".
- 15-past, guide: बोलना is described as "normally used without ने". Usage with ने (उसने झूठ बोला) also
  exists when there is an object.
- 18-want-need-can, guide: plural चाहिएँ is mentioned as the careful written form; many style guides use
  चाहिए for both.
- 19-have, guide: "मेरे एक भाई है" (invariable मेरे for relatives) is mentioned as a common variant.
- 21-food, "एक रोटी और दीजिए।": chosen to avoid the plural (दो रोटियाँ / colloquial दो रोटी).
- 22-directions, "मुझे हवाई अड्डे जाना है।": existing phrase; English changed from "I want to go" to
  "I need to go".
- 25-time, "बस कब निकलेगी?": English changed from "When does the bus leave?" to "When will the bus leave?".
- 29-travel, "मुझे दिल्ली का एक टिकट चाहिए।": टिकट is masculine for some speakers and feminine for others
  (दिल्ली की एक टिकट). The guide says so.
- 30-work-study and 31-conversation, "मुझे (यह) समझ नहीं आया।": existing phrase. Very common, but समझ is
  feminine, so some speakers say समझ नहीं आई; the fully standard form मेरी समझ में नहीं आया is given in the
  guide. English changed from "I don't understand" to "I didn't understand".
- 32-feelings, "मैं आपसे सहमत हूँ।": correct but formal; the note gives the conversational alternative.
- 35-dialogue-market, "भैया, आम कैसे दिए?": idiomatic market Hindi; the textbook alternative
  आम कितने के हैं? is in the guide.
- 35-dialogue-market, "ये रहे आपके तीस रुपये।": idiomatic "here are your ..."; glossed literally as
  "these stayed".
- 23-shopping: "यह बहुत महँगा है।" is translated "This is too expensive" (existing); 12-adjectives explains
  that बहुत covers both "very" and "too".

- 22-directions, guide: "थोड़ा दूर है" (adverbial थोड़ा) is given as an answer you may hear; थोड़ी दूर है, matching
  कितनी दूर, is at least as common. Left as is.
- 26-weather, guide: "बर्फ़ गिर रही है" for "it is snowing"; बर्फ़ पड़ रही है is equally usual.
- 28-phone: हेलो is spelt this way throughout; हैलो is also common.
- 00-course, `about`: रुपया is romanised `rupayaa` (three syllables); in fast speech it is closer to `rupyaa`.

## Structure

- 35 units rather than "about 30": the curriculum list gives 33 topics, plus two dialogue units.
- Cross-unit repeats are intentional (e.g. "कोई बात नहीं।" in 03 and 31, "मीटर से चलिए।" in 22 and 35); each
  is romanised identically.
